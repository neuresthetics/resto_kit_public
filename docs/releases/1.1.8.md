## Crawlspace negative air and containment

- Insulation removal now includes negative air and containment guidance. Pulling and bagging insulation puts dust and fibers in the air, so keep the crawlspace negative (fan inside ducted out a foundation vent, or hatch containment with an air scrubber inside) and never blow air into the crawl through a vent. On occupied homes, set hatch containment before the first bag comes out, and deal with crawl-connected HVAC first.
- Special contamination (sewage and heavy contamination) now includes negative air guidance through bulk removal, liming, and cleaning. Hatch containment with an air scrubber is the stronger setup for sewage, and exhaust stays clear of windows, doors, and intakes. Active heavy sewage is extracted first before negative isolation, with containment first only in the listed exceptions.

## Vapor barrier footings, 12-inch rule, and work order

- New Posts, Piers, and Footings guidance: lay visqueen around each post footing, cut low and away from the wood so the barrier stops on the concrete short of the post-to-footing gap. Wrap and seal concrete piers and footings to masonry only, never to wood, and photograph any rot or wet wood at post bases. These footing details are common practice, not a code citation.
- Vapor barrier overlap and stem-wall height are updated from 6 inches to 12 inches, with a note explaining that 12 inches meets Oregon code (2021 Oregon Residential Specialty Code R408.1, carried into the 2026 code as R408.2.1 effective October 1, 2026, plus Oregon's unvented crawl amendment R408.3) and goes beyond the 6-inch national model code. The kit stays jurisdiction-neutral and still asks for the work state.
- The crawlspace order line now reads: insulation removal before vapor barrier (dirty visqueen out, clean visqueen down before new insulation).
