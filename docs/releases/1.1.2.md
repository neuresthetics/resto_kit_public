## Pre-setup power survey

- Before setup, run a short power survey of the circuits near the work area so you know whether a power box is needed.
- Project the equipment from the job conditions, then add up the nameplate amps of each unit. A typical large dehumidifier draws roughly 6 to 8 amps and an axial air mover roughly 1 to 3 amps, but always use the nameplate.
- Survey only the nearby circuits: photograph the panel, note breaker sizes and ground-fault breakers, map outlets to breakers, and note existing loads.
- Keep continuous load to about 12 amps on a 15 amp circuit and 16 amps on a 20 amp circuit.
- Use a power box only when the projected load is more than the free capacity, or when house circuits in the work zone are off, wet, or not trusted. Do not default to a power box on every job. The survey goes in the job file as the justification.

## Electricity usage record for the homeowner

- Tell the homeowner up front about power use.
- Give them a simple usage record of each unit, its nameplate amps and voltage (usually 120 volts), and the dates it was placed and removed or the days it ran.
- Energy is estimated as amps times volts times hours, divided by 1000, in kilowatt-hours. If a unit ran around the clock, hours are the days run times 24.
- The homeowner or insurer applies their own power company's rate. We do not quote rates, and we do not promise that reimbursement will be covered.
